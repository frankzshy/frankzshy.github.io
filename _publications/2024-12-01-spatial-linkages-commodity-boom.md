With Felipe Benguria and Felipe Saffie. This paper studies the uneven effects of a commodity boom across workers, by skill, and across regions. We develop a dynamic quantitative model of an economy with many regions connected through interregional trade and migration, and apply it to Brazil's commodity boom of the 2000s. At the aggregate level, the boom leads to a decline in the skill premium, driven by a larger increase in the real wages of unskilled relative to skilled workers; the commodity boom accounts for about a quarter of the observed decline. Spatial linkages — interregional migration and trade — play an important role in shaping the impact of the boom, reducing the decline in the national skill premium while also reducing spatial inequality.

Presented at: Trade and Uneven Development Conference (2024, coauthor presented), Southern Economic Association 92nd Annual Meeting (2022).
