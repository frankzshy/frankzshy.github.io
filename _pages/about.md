I am an Economist in the Research Department at the [International Monetary Fund](https://www.imf.org), where I work on the DIGNAD model for country-specific policy analysis and collaborate with country teams on debt sustainability assessments under natural disasters. I hold a Ph.D. in Economics from the [University of Maryland, College Park](https://econ.umd.edu) (2022).

My research interests are in international trade, with a focus on global supply chains and trade policy, and secondary interests in international finance and macroeconomics. My work uses quantitative general equilibrium models to study how trade shocks, tariffs, and commodity booms propagate through supply chains and across regions and workers.

Before joining the IMF, I was a Principal Associate / Quantitative Analyst at [Capital One](https://www.capitalone.com), where I contributed to consumer credit loss forecasting models. I previously interned at the IMF's Fund Internship Program and was a Research Assistant to Prof. Eunhee Lee at the University of Maryland and to Dr. Jun Ma at the Research Bureau of the People's Bank of China.

Research
======
**Primary field:** International Trade
**Secondary fields:** International Finance, Macroeconomics

My current research studies:
* How trade shocks and tariffs propagate through global supply chains and affect welfare and sectoral outcomes.
* How trade agreements affect global value chains beyond tariff liberalization.
* How commodity booms have uneven effects across workers and regions through spatial linkages such as interregional trade and migration.

See the [Publications](/publications/) page for the full list of papers and working papers.
